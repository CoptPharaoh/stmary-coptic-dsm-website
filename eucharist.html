The Sacrament of the Holy Eucharist

What Is the Eucharist?

The Holy Eucharist, meaning "Thanksgiving," is one of the seven Holy Mysteries of the Coptic Orthodox Church and stands at the center of Orthodox Christian worship.

Through this Mystery, the faithful truly partake of the Body and Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ under the appearances of bread and wine. During the Divine Liturgy, through the Eucharistic prayers and the invocation of the Holy Spirit, the bread and wine become the true Body and Blood of Christ.

The Eucharist is not merely a symbolic remembrance. It is a real and sacramental communion with the crucified and risen Lord. Through Holy Communion, believers are united with Christ, strengthened in spiritual life, and brought into deeper communion with His Church.

"He who eats My flesh and drinks My blood abides in Me, and I in him."

John 6:56

Old Testament Foreshadowing

Promises Fulfilled in Christ

The Church reads these biblical images in the light of Christ, who fulfills God's saving promises and gives Himself as the Bread of Life.

The Tree of Life

Genesis 3:22; Revelation 2:7; Revelation 22:2

In Eden, the Tree of Life represented the life and immortality God intended for humanity. After the Fall, Adam and Eve were prevented from eating from it.

Christian tradition understands the Cross as the life-giving Tree through which humanity receives the promise of eternal life. The Eucharist shares in this restoration: Christ gives Himself as the Bread of Life. Revelation's Tree of Life points toward the fullness of life in God's Kingdom.

The Passover Lamb

Exodus 12; John 1:29; 1 Corinthians 5:7

At the first Passover, the Israelites sacrificed a lamb without blemish and ate its flesh. Its blood marked their deliverance from judgment.

The Church recognizes a foreshadowing of Jesus Christ, the true Lamb of God. Through His sacrifice, Christ delivers humanity from sin and death; the Eucharist brings believers into communion with the sacrificed and risen Lamb.

"Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us." 1 Corinthians 5:7

Manna in the Wilderness

Exodus 16; John 6:49-51

God sustained Israel in the wilderness with manna from heaven. Jesus revealed Himself as the true Bread from heaven: manna sustained earthly life, but Christ offers eternal life.

"I am the living bread which came down from heaven. If anyone eats of this bread, he will live forever." John 6:51

The Eucharist fulfills this imagery through communion with the true Bread of Life.

Melchizedek's Offering

Genesis 14:18; Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 7:1-3

Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, brought out bread and wine when he blessed Abraham.

Christian tradition understands him as a type of Christ, the eternal High Priest. His offering of bread and wine has also been interpreted as foreshadowing the Eucharistic offering. Christ fulfills this priesthood through His eternal ministry and saving sacrifice.

Institution of the Holy Eucharist

The Last Supper

At the Mystical Supper, before His Passion, our Lord Jesus Christ instituted the Holy Eucharist. He entrusted this Mystery to His apostles, and the Church has continued celebrating it through the Divine Liturgy.

Matthew 26:26

"Take, eat; this is My body."

Matthew 26:27-28

"Drink from it, all of you. For this is My blood of the new covenant, which is shed for many for the remission of sins."

Read also Mark 14:22-24; Luke 22:19-20; 1 Corinthians 11:23-26.

Apostolic Teaching

"The cup of blessing which we bless, is it not the communion of the blood of Christ? The bread which we break, is it not the communion of the body of Christ?"

1 Corinthians 10:16

St. Paul teaches that the Eucharist is true participation in Christ. Holy Communion also expresses and strengthens the unity of His Church.

"For we, though many, are one bread and one body; for we all partake of that one bread."

1 Corinthians 10:17

The Eucharist and Salvation

The Bread of Eternal Life

"Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you have no life in you. Whoever eats My flesh and drinks My blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up at the last day."

John 6:53-54

The Eucharist is intimately connected with the Christian life of salvation. Through Holy Communion, the faithful receive Christ Himself and participate in His life.

Union with Christ

Christ abides in the faithful, and the faithful abide in Him.

Sanctification

The Eucharist strengthens believers in their ongoing life of repentance and holiness.

Unity of the Church

The faithful are united as members of the one Body of Christ.

Hope of Resurrection

Holy Communion directs believers toward the resurrection and eternal life promised by Christ.

Participation in Divine Life

Through grace, believers become partakers of the divine nature, as described in 2 Peter 1:4. This participation is a gift of grace; it does not mean becoming divine by essence.

The Church Fathers on the Real Presence

The Ancient Eucharistic Faith

These summaries present the teaching of four early Christian witnesses, with references to their writings.

St. Ignatius of Antioch

Ignatius identifies the Eucharist with the flesh of Jesus Christ, who suffered and rose again, and challenges those who reject this confession. His early second-century witness shows that belief in Christ's real Eucharistic Body belongs to the ancient faith of the Church.

Letter to the Smyrnaeans, chapter 7

Read the source

St. Justin Martyr

Justin explains that Christians receive the Eucharistic food as the flesh and blood of the incarnate Jesus, rather than ordinary bread and drink. His account records the early Church's Eucharistic faith and its connection to the Incarnation.

First Apology, chapter 66

Read the source

St. Cyril of Jerusalem

Cyril teaches believers to trust Christ's declaration that the consecrated gifts are His Body and Blood. Their faith rests on the Lord's words, even when the senses perceive bread and wine.

Mystagogical Catechesis 4 (Catechetical Lecture 22)

St. John Chrysostom

Chrysostom emphasizes how intimately Christ joins the faithful to Himself through His Body and Blood. Such communion calls believers to love, gratitude, and reverence as they approach the holy gifts.

Christology and the Eucharist

Communion with the One Incarnate Lord

The Coptic Orthodox Church confesses that Jesus Christ is the One Incarnate Nature of God the Word, fully divine and fully human. His divinity and humanity are united without mingling, confusion, alteration, or separation.

In the Eucharist, believers receive the true Body and Blood of the one Lord Jesus Christ. The Church does not separate Christ's humanity from His divinity: the Eucharist is communion with the incarnate, crucified, and risen Savior.

The faithful participate in God's life through grace, without becoming divine by essence.

The Eucharist as Sacrifice

The One Sacrifice of Christ

The Eucharist is both Holy Communion and the Church's sacramental participation in Christ's saving sacrifice. Christ offered Himself once for all through His death upon the Cross.

The Divine Liturgy does not repeat His crucifixion. Through the Eucharistic Mystery, the Church commemorates and participates in the saving reality of His one sacrifice.

Hebrews 9:11-15

Christ enters as High Priest through His own blood, obtaining eternal redemption.

Hebrews 9:24-28

Christ appears before God for us and was offered once to bear the sins of many.

Hebrews 10:10-14

The offering of Christ's Body is once for all; His one sacrifice is the foundation of our sanctification.

Hebrews 13:10

The apostolic witness speaks of the altar and participation in the holy offering.

Anamnesis

Anamnesis means remembrance or memorial. In the Divine Liturgy, remembrance is more than simply recalling a past event: the Church prayerfully commemorates Christ's saving Passion, Resurrection, Ascension, and promised Second Coming.

The Eucharist unites thanksgiving, remembrance, sacrifice, and communion.

The Eucharist in the Divine Liturgy

Thanksgiving, Remembrance, and Communion

The Eucharistic Mystery belongs to the complete liturgical action. These principal elements help us follow the Church's prayer; the institution narrative, anamnesis, and epiclesis belong within the anaphora.

  1. 1. Preparation of the OfferingThe bread and wine are prepared and presented for the Eucharistic celebration.
  2. 2. The Liturgy of the WordThe Church hears the Holy Scriptures and proclaims the Gospel.
  3. 3. The AnaphoraThe Church offers thanksgiving and praise to God.
  4. 4. The Institution NarrativeThe Church recalls the words of Christ at the Mystical Supper.
  5. 5. The AnamnesisThe Church commemorates the saving work of Christ.
  6. 6. The EpiclesisThe Church invokes the Holy Spirit upon the Eucharistic offering.
  7. 7. Holy CommunionThe faithful who are properly prepared receive the Holy Body and Precious Blood of Christ.

The Priestly Confession Before Communion

The Confession of Faith

Before distributing Holy Communion, the priest solemnly confesses the Church's faith in the true Body and Blood of Christ.

"Amen. Amen. Amen. I believe, I believe, I believe and confess to the last breath..."

Opening excerpt from the liturgical Confession of Faith

This confession affirms:

  • The reality of Christ's life-giving Body.
  • His Incarnation from the Virgin Mary.
  • The inseparable union of His divinity and humanity.
  • His voluntary sacrifice upon the Cross.
  • The Eucharist as given for salvation, remission of sins, and eternal life.

For the complete prayer, follow the approved Coptic Orthodox liturgical text used by the parish.

The Holy Bread: Qurbana

Bread Offered for the Eucharist

In the Coptic Orthodox tradition, the Eucharistic bread is commonly called qurbana, meaning an offering. The Church uses leavened bread prepared for the Divine Liturgy.

The Offering of Bread

The Church presents bread in thanksgiving, offering the fruits of creation and human labor to God.

Resurrection Life

The Church associates the living, leavened bread with the life-giving Resurrection of Christ. This symbolism does not depend on a conclusive claim about the exact historical timing of the Last Supper.

Reverence for the Gifts

The bread and wine are prepared and handled with care because they are offered for the holy Mystery.

One Church in Christ

The one bread recalls the unity of the many faithful in the one Body of Christ.

Preparation for Holy Communion

Preparing to Receive Christ

Holy Communion calls for spiritual preparation, repentance, and reverence. Preparation is not a claim of personal perfection; it is a response of faith, humility, repentance, and obedience.

Baptism and Chrismation

The Eucharist belongs to the sacramental life of those received into the Orthodox Church.

Repentance and Confession

Believers examine themselves, repent of their sins, and follow the Church's sacramental discipline concerning Confession.

The Life of RepentancePrepare for Confession

Reconciliation

Seek peace with others. Do not approach the Holy Mysteries while deliberately holding hatred or refusing reconciliation.

Prayer and Fasting

Prayer and Eucharistic fasting prepare the faithful to receive with attention and reverence. Specific fasting requirements follow the guidance of the Church and one's father of confession.

Participation in the Divine Liturgy

Participate prayerfully and attentively in the worship of the Church.

St. Paul's Warning

In 1 Corinthians 11:27-29, St. Paul warns against receiving the Body and Blood in an unworthy manner. He calls the faithful to self-examination and discernment of the Lord's Body, approaching with reverence rather than carelessness.

Visitors who are not communicants of the Coptic Orthodox Church should speak with the priest about the Church's practice and the path toward full sacramental communion.

Final Spiritual Reflection

The Medicine of Immortality

The Holy Eucharist is a profound gift of God's love and a source of spiritual life. Through this Mystery, Christ invites His people into communion with Himself.

The faithful approach the Holy Mysteries with thanksgiving, repentance, humility, reverence, and joy. Holy Communion strengthens the Christian journey and directs believers toward the hope of resurrection and eternal life.

"He who eats My flesh and drinks My blood abides in Me, and I in him."

John 6:56
