The Sacrament of the Unction of the Sick

What Is the Unction of the Sick?

The Unction of the Sick is one of the seven Holy Mysteries of the Coptic Orthodox Church. Through this sacrament, the Church prays for those suffering from illness, asking God for mercy, forgiveness, spiritual strength, and healing according to His divine will.

The sacrament involves the blessing of oil, readings from Holy Scripture, prayers offered by the priest, and anointing in the name of the Lord. Human beings are created with both body and soul; healing encompasses the whole person.

This is a sacramental ministry through which the Church invokes God's grace upon those who suffer, not merely a symbolic ceremony.

Although the Church prays for physical recovery, the sacrament does not guarantee that every illness will be cured. God's grace may be manifested through physical healing, spiritual restoration, forgiveness, consolation, endurance, and peace.

Christ is the true Physician of our souls and bodies.

Biblical Foundations

Prayer, Anointing, and Mercy

The Prayer of Faith

"Is anyone among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord."

James 5:14-15

St. James instructs the sick to call the presbyters to pray and anoint them. He connects the prayer of faith with God's healing mercy and forgiveness. This is the principal New Testament foundation for sacramental anointing.

The Apostles Anoint the Sick

"And they cast out many demons, and anointed with oil many who were sick, and healed them."

Mark 6:13

The apostles' ministry joins prayer, anointing, and healing. The Church continues to pray for the sick in faithfulness to Christ's command and their example.

Christ's Command to Heal

"Heal the sick, cleanse the lepers, raise the dead, cast out demons. Freely you have received, freely give."

Matthew 10:8

Christ entrusted His disciples with a ministry of mercy and healing. The Church's compassionate care continues this mission.

Oil as an Instrument of Care

Luke 10:34

The Good Samaritan tends the wounded man's injuries with oil and wine. The parable illustrates the biblical association of oil with compassionate care and healing; it is not itself the institution of the sacrament.

Christ, the Physician of Souls and Bodies

The Healing Ministry of Jesus Christ

Throughout the Gospels, Christ reveals God's compassion toward people suffering from sickness, disability, and spiritual affliction. His miracles show His authority over illness and concern for the whole person.

Read Matthew 4:23-24; Matthew 8:1-17; Mark 2:1-12; Luke 5:17-26.

Healing the Whole Person

In the account of the paralytic, Christ declares forgiveness and then heals the man's physical condition. His saving ministry encompasses spiritual and bodily restoration.

Compassion Without Judgment

Physical illness should not automatically be interpreted as punishment for personal sin. In John 9:1-3, Christ rejects that explanation for the man born blind.

The Church's Ministry of Healing

The Coptic Orthodox Church continues Christ's compassion through prayer, sacramental anointing, pastoral care, and practical assistance. God remains present with His people in sickness and suffering.

The Church Fathers on Healing and Anointing

These summaries draw on identifiable writings. The Fathers' broader teaching on healing and priestly prayer should be distinguished from the later details of the Coptic liturgical rite.

St. John Chrysostom

Chrysostom discusses priestly ministry as a responsibility for spiritual restoration. In his account of forgiveness after Baptism, he invokes James's teaching about prayer for the sick and anointing. He directs attention to God's mercy and the sacred responsibility entrusted to the Church.

St. Cyril of Alexandria

In his commentary on Luke, Cyril presents the incarnate Word as humanity's Savior. His treatment of Christ's healing of the paralytic connects the Lord's authority to forgive with His power to heal. This offers a theological foundation for understanding restoration as the work of Christ's divine grace.

Early Christian Practice

Prayer for the sick and anointing with oil are witnessed in Mark 6:13 and James 5:14-15. Chrysostom's discussion shows that this biblical witness remained part of early Christian teaching.

The later arrangement of seven prayers in the Coptic rite should not be read back into every detail of these early accounts.

The Coptic Orthodox Rite of Unction

The Sacrament of the Lamp

The Unction of the Sick is associated with the rite known as the Prayer of the Lamp, or Qandil. The service includes prayers, Scripture readings, and the blessing of oil, invoking God's mercy and healing.

The Seven Prayers

The traditional rite includes seven prayers and Scripture readings. Seven expresses fullness and completeness in the Church's prayer for healing, forgiveness, protection, and spiritual strength.

The Blessed Oil

Oil is blessed through the prayers of the Church and used for anointing, expressing prayer for the healing and sanctification of the person receiving the sacrament.

The Sign of the Cross

The anointing is performed reverently in the name of the Lord. The sign of the Cross directs the faithful to Christ, whose saving Passion and Resurrection ground our hope.

The Ministry of the Priest

A priest administers the sacrament according to the Coptic liturgical order. Multiple priests may participate, but seven priests are not always required. Pastoral arrangements follow the Church's guidance and the sick person's circumstances.

When Is the Sacrament Celebrated?

Accompanying the Faithful in Illness

During Illness

The faithful may request the sacrament during serious illness or significant health difficulties, seeking physical healing, spiritual restoration, and peace.

Before Major Medical Procedures

Those preparing for surgery or significant treatment may seek the Church's prayers and anointing for comfort and strength amid uncertainty.

During Prolonged Suffering

Those with chronic illness or prolonged suffering may seek pastoral guidance concerning the sacrament. The Church continues to accompany them with prayer, compassion, and care.

Near the End of Life

The sacrament may be celebrated for those seriously ill near the end of life, but it is not exclusively for the dying. Its purpose is healing, forgiveness, strengthening, and communion with God's grace.

General Unction Before Holy Week

In the Coptic Orthodox tradition, General Unction is celebrated on the last Friday of Great Lent, before Lazarus Saturday and Holy Week. It brings the congregation together in prayer for healing and spiritual strength as the Church approaches Pascha.

Consult the parish for its actual arrangements and service times.

Physical Healing and Spiritual Restoration

Healing According to God's Will

The Church prays with faith that God is able to heal every illness. The sacrament is not a guarantee of immediate or complete physical recovery: grace may be experienced through healing, consolation, patience, courage, and spiritual renewal.

Physical Healing

The Church asks God to restore health and relieve suffering according to His will.

Spiritual Healing

The sacrament calls believers to turn toward Christ in repentance, faith, and prayer.

Forgiveness and Reconciliation

James 5:15 connects the prayer of faith with forgiveness of sins. Unction expresses God's mercy while remaining distinct from the Sacrament of Repentance and Confession.

Strength in Suffering

The sacrament encourages hope in hardship. Suffering is not inherently good; Christians respond to those who suffer with compassion rather than judgment.

The Incarnation and the Healing of Humanity

Christ Restores the Whole Person

The Coptic Orthodox Church confesses Jesus Christ as the One Incarnate Nature of God the Word, fully divine and fully human. Through the Incarnation, the Son of God truly assumed human nature for our salvation.

His healing ministry reveals compassion for the whole person. The Church's sacramental ministry reflects salvation as the restoration of humanity through God's grace.

The healing sought through Unction is grounded in Christ's saving work. Our confession of the Incarnation does not imply a guarantee of physical recovery.

Faith, Medicine, and the Care of the Sick

Prayer and Medical Treatment

Seeking appropriate medical care is compatible with Christian faith. The Church's sacramental prayers are not a replacement for medical treatment; the faithful may receive treatment while also seeking prayer and sacramental ministry.

God's care may be experienced through physicians, caregivers, medicine, family support, and the prayers of the faithful.

Supporting Those Who Suffer

  • Pray for the sick.
  • Offer compassionate visits.
  • Provide practical assistance.
  • Give encouragement.
  • Respect each person's dignity.
  • Support families and caregivers.

Those wishing to receive the Unction of the Sick should contact their parish priest for guidance.

Final Spiritual Reflection

Christ, the Physician of Our Souls and Bodies

The Sacrament of the Unction of the Sick reveals the compassion and mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ. Through the Church's prayers and anointing with blessed oil, the faithful seek healing grace, forgiveness, consolation, and strength.

In illness, uncertainty, and suffering, Christians are reminded that they are not abandoned. Christ remains present with His people, offering hope, peace, and the promise of eternal life.
