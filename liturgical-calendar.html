The Coptic Orthodox Liturgical Calendar

Sanctifying Time Through Feasts, Fasts, and Prayer

Through feasts, fasts, and the remembrance of the saints, the Church invites us to participate in the saving work of Jesus Christ. The calendar is more than a schedule: it gives shape to a life of prayer, repentance, thanksgiving, and communion with God.

Each year leads us back to the Incarnation, Crucifixion, Resurrection, Ascension, and the coming of the Holy Spirit.

03 / The Church Year

An Ancient Egyptian Calendar in the Life of the Church

The Coptic calendar grew out of Egypt's ancient calendar tradition and became part of the worship of Christian Egypt. It helps order fixed feasts, fasting periods, and commemorations alongside the movable cycle centered on the Resurrection.

The Era of the Martyrs begins in AD 284, the year of Diocletian's accession. The era is named in remembrance of the Christian witnesses associated with the persecutions of his reign, not because those persecutions began on the first day of the era.

Ancient Roots

A calendar inherited from ancient Egypt and received into the life of the Church.

Thirteen Months

Twelve months have thirty days; Nasie has five, or six in a Coptic leap year.

Era of the Martyrs

A.M. stands for Anno Martyrum. The numbering recalls the enduring witness of the martyrs.

Liturgical Life

The calendar helps locate observances and readings; it is not a parish service schedule.

Source: Midlands Coptic Orthodox Diocese: Coptic Calendar.

05 / The Church Year

The Rhythm of the Liturgical Year

Fasting and feasting are complementary. The fast makes room for prayer, self-examination, and charity; the feast calls the Church to joy and thanksgiving. The following guide follows the rhythm of the year. It groups months, feasts, and periods of worship without treating them all as equivalent formal seasons.

Coptic New Year

Nayrouz begins on 1 Tout. The Church gives thanks for a new year and remembers the courage of the martyrs.

Kiahk

The praises of this month prepare the Church for the Nativity, with particular attention to the Incarnation and the Theotokos.

Nativity and Theophany

The Church celebrates the birth of Christ and His Baptism in the Jordan. These are distinct feasts, not a single observance.

Great Lent

The preparatory week and the forty-day fast train the heart in repentance and prepare it for Pascha.

Holy Week

The Church follows Christ's Passion through Scripture, hymns, and the prayers of Holy Pascha.

Resurrection

Pascha is the central feast: Christ rises from the dead and opens the way to new life.

Holy Fifty Days

The joyful Paschal period includes the Ascension and culminates in Pentecost.

Apostles' Fast

After Pentecost, the Church enters a time of prayer and fasting associated with apostolic service.

Fast of St. Mary

This fast honors the Theotokos and invites the faithful to imitate her obedience and trust in Christ.

06 / The Church Year

The Seven Major Feasts of the Lord

These feasts proclaim central events in Christ's saving work. A traditional Coptic date does not always settle the order of liturgical observance when feasts coincide.

29 Baramhat / traditional date

The Annunciation

Gabriel announces that Mary will bear Jesus Christ. Her faithful response belongs to the mystery of the Word becoming flesh. Luke 1:26–38.

29 Kiahk / see leap-year exception

The Nativity

The eternal Son enters human life in humility. The traditional date is 29 Kiahk; observance on 28 Kiahk following a Coptic leap year accounts for the relevant calendar alignment. Luke 2:1–20; Matthew 1:18–25.

11 Toba

The Theophany

At Christ's Baptism in the Jordan, the Son is baptized, the Father's voice is heard, and the Spirit descends. Matthew 3:13–17.

Seven days before Pascha

Palm Sunday

Christ enters Jerusalem and is received with cries of Hosanna. The feast leads into Holy Week. Matthew 21:1–11.

Movable / Pascha

The Resurrection

Christ rises from the dead. Pascha is the central feast of the year and the foundation of Christian hope. Matthew 28:1–10; 1 Corinthians 15:20–22.

Paschal day 40

The Ascension

The risen Lord ascends. The Church celebrates on the fortieth day, counting Pascha as day one. Acts 1:9–11.

Paschal day 50

Pentecost

The Holy Spirit descends upon the Apostles, equipping them to bear witness to Christ. Acts 2:1–4.

Source: Coptic Orthodox Diocese of Mississauga, Vancouver and Western Canada.

Coptic Heritage: Kiahk and the Nativity date. The interactive calendar omits the Annunciation rather than implying its traditional date overrides Holy Week.

07 / The Church Year

The Seven Minor Feasts of the Lord

“Minor” is a traditional classification, not a judgment about the significance of these events. Each feast invites attention to a particular moment in the Gospel.

6 Toba

Circumcision of the Lord

Christ is circumcised on the eighth day. Luke 2:21.

8 Amshir

Entrance into the Temple

Mary and Joseph present Christ in the Temple, where Simeon and Anna bear witness to Him. Luke 2:22–38.

24 Bashans

Flight into Egypt

The Holy Family finds refuge in Egypt. This Gospel event holds a special place in the memory of the Coptic Church. Matthew 2:13–15.

13 Toba

Wedding at Cana of Galilee

Christ turns water into wine, the first sign recorded in John's Gospel. John 2:1–11.

13 Mesra

Transfiguration

Christ reveals His glory to His disciples on the mountain. Matthew 17:1–8.

Thursday before Pascha

Covenant Thursday

The Church remembers the Last Supper and the institution of the Eucharist. Matthew 26:26–29.

Sunday after Pascha

Thomas Sunday

The risen Christ meets St. Thomas, whose confession calls the faithful to faith. John 20:24–29.

Source: Coptic Orthodox Diocese of Mississauga, Vancouver and Western Canada.

08 / The Church Year

Fasting, Prayer, and Repentance

Fasting is not simply a change of diet. Joined to prayer, repentance, and acts of charity, it helps us turn our attention toward God and our neighbor. Read Matthew 6:16–18, Isaiah 58:6–7, and Joel 2:12–13.

Great Lent

The preparatory week and forty-day fast lead toward Pascha. Holy Week follows as a distinct period of Passion worship.

Fast of the Nativity

The Church prepares to celebrate Christ's birth. The praises of Kiahk give voice to this expectation.

Fast of the Apostles

Begins after Pentecost; its length varies with Pascha. The Church remembers the Apostles' prayer, mission, and service.

Fast of St. Mary

A season of prayer and fasting associated with devotion to the Theotokos.

Fast of Nineveh

A three-day fast recalling the repentance of Nineveh in the Book of Jonah.

Wednesday and Friday Fasts

Weekly observances remember Christ's betrayal and Crucifixion, subject to liturgical exceptions, including the Holy Fifty Days.

Holy Week

The Passion days form a particularly solemn period of prayer and fasting, distinct from the preceding forty days.

Ask your father of confession about personal fasting practice. This overview does not prescribe fasting hours, dispensations, or individual dietary rules.

Source: Coptic Orthodox diocesan guide to feasts and fasts.

09 / The Church Year

The Journey of Great Lent

The Coptic lectionary distinguishes the Sunday before the fast from the first six Sundays of Lent. Palm Sunday follows them and opens the Holy Week guide. The Gospel cycle moves from preparation and temptation toward return, healing, and sight.

Before the fast

Preparation Sunday

Prayer, almsgiving, and fasting are offered to God rather than practiced for public praise. Matthew 6:1–18.

First Sunday

Treasures

Christ calls us to seek the Kingdom and place our treasure in heaven. Matthew 6:19–33.

Second Sunday

Temptation

Christ resists temptation in the wilderness. Matthew 4:1–11.

Third Sunday

Prodigal Son

The Father's welcome gives hope to those who turn back in repentance. Luke 15:11–32.

Fourth Sunday

Samaritan Woman

Christ offers living water and teaches worship in spirit and truth. John 4:1–42.

Fifth Sunday

Paralytic

Christ heals the man at the pool. John 5:1–18.

Sixth Sunday

Man Born Blind

Christ gives sight, and the man comes to confess Him. John 9:1–41.

Following Sunday

Palm Sunday

The entry into Jerusalem leads the Church from the Lenten Sunday cycle into Holy Week. Matthew 21:1–11.

Source: Coptic Lenten Katameros (PDF).

10 / The Church Year

Holy Week: Walking with Christ to the Cross

Through Scripture, hymns, and the prayers of Holy Pascha, the Church contemplates the Passion, death, and burial of Christ. The guide below includes the Resurrection as the culmination, not as another day of mourning.

  1. Palm Sunday

    Christ enters Jerusalem.

  2. Holy Monday

    The Church continues its contemplation of Christ's final days.

  3. Holy Tuesday

    The faithful listen to the appointed Passion readings.

  4. Holy Wednesday

    The Church remembers events leading toward Christ's Passion.

  5. Covenant Thursday

    The Last Supper and institution of the Eucharist.

  6. Good Friday

    The Crucifixion and death of Jesus Christ.

  7. Bright Saturday

    The solemn vigil in anticipation of the Resurrection.

  8. Feast of the Resurrection

    The joyful celebration of Christ's victory over death.

11 / The Church Year

The Joy of the Resurrection

From Pascha through Pentecost the Church celebrates Christ's victory over death. This is not simply a pause between fasts. It is a time to give thanks for new life in the risen Christ.

Pascha

The risen Christ meets His disciples and grants peace. John 20:19–29.

The Ascension

Christ ascends on the fortieth day of the Paschal period. Acts 1:9–11.

Pentecost

On the fiftieth day the Holy Spirit descends, and the Church is sent to witness. Acts 2:1–4.

Source: Fr. Tadros Malaty on the Holy Fifty Days.

12 / The Church Year

Remembering the Saints and Martyrs

The Synaxarium preserves commemorations of saints, martyrs, and events in the life of the Church. It is commonly read alongside the appointed liturgical readings, but it is not itself a book of Scripture.

The martyrs' witness has shaped Coptic Christian memory. Their lives are remembered not simply as stories of suffering, but as examples of faithfulness to Christ. Consult the appointed Synaxarium and service books rather than assuming a daily commemoration from this calendar.

St. Mark the Evangelist

Remembered for bringing the Gospel to Alexandria.

St. Athanasius the Apostolic

An Alexandrian bishop and defender of the confession that the Son is truly God.

St. Anthony the Great

A witness to prayer and repentance in the Egyptian desert.

St. Cyril of Alexandria

A defender of the unity of the incarnate Word.

St. Mary the Theotokos

Honored for her faithful response to God and as the Mother of God.

        15 / The Church Year

        A Year of Spiritual Growth

        The Church year helps us live the faith throughout the year. Fasting makes room for repentance and care for others. Feasting directs our joy toward God. Neither is complete if it leaves our relationship with God and our neighbor unchanged. Ecclesiastes 3:1; Romans 12:1–2; Colossians 3:17.

        Remembrance

        Return to the events of Christ's saving work through the Church's Scripture readings and prayer.

        Repentance

        Let the fast uncover habits that need healing, and turn back to God without despair.

        Thanksgiving

        Celebrate God's gifts and share the joy of the feast with others.

        Spiritual Renewal

        Bring what you hear in worship into the choices and relationships of daily life.

        18 / The Church Year

        Questions About the Coptic Calendar

        01Why does the Coptic Church use a different calendar?

        It preserves an ancient Egyptian calendar tradition received into Coptic liturgical life.

        02What does A.M. mean?

        Anno Martyrum, or Era of the Martyrs.

        03How many months are in the Coptic year?

        Thirteen: twelve thirty-day months and the short month of Nasie.

        04Why does the Coptic New Year fall in September?

        The inherited Egyptian calendar starts with 1 Tout, which falls in September in the current Gregorian alignment.

        05Why do some feast dates change each year?

        Movable feasts depend on the annual calculation of Pascha rather than a fixed Coptic month and day.

        06Why do Coptic Christians fast?

        Fasting helps turn the heart toward God when joined to prayer, repentance, and charity.

        07What is the Synaxarium?

        A collection of commemorations of saints, martyrs, and events in Church history.

        08What are the Holy Fifty Days?

        The joyful period from the Resurrection through Pentecost.

        09Are all fasting rules the same for everyone?

        Personal fasting practice may require pastoral guidance and consideration of individual circumstances.

        20 / The Church Year

        Every Season Leads Us Toward Christ

        The feasts proclaim the joy of salvation. The fasts call us toward repentance and renewal. The commemorations keep alive the memory of those who followed Christ faithfully. Together, they invite us to grow in prayer and communion with God throughout the year.
