Learn the Coptic Alphabet

The Bohairic teaching sequence contains 24 Greek-derived letters, seven Egyptian-derived characters (Ϣ, Ϥ, Ϧ, Ϩ, Ϫ, Ϭ, Ϯ), and Sou, a historical numeral for six. The 31 phonetic letters represent Egyptian, including vowels. Inventories vary by dialect.

From Ancient Egyptian to Coptic

Coptic is the latest historical stage of Egyptian. Its Greek-derived alphabet and Greek loanwords reflect contact with Greek. Egyptian is the language itself, Old Egyptian is one historical stage, and hieroglyphic and hieratic are writing systems.

  1. Old Egyptian

    Third millennium BC, including the Pyramid Texts.

  2. Middle Egyptian

    A classical literary stage from the early second millennium BC, retained in writing as speech changed.

  3. Late Egyptian

    A later stage particularly associated with the New Kingdom.

  4. Demotic Egyptian

    A language stage and the name of a script used from the first millennium BC into the Roman period.

  5. Early Coptic

    Egyptian writers experimented with Greek-based alphabetic writing before a literary standard took shape.

  6. Christian Coptic

    Late antique biblical translations, sermons, and monastic literature expanded the written tradition.

  7. Medieval Coptic

    Arabic gradually became dominant in everyday life at different rates across regions.

  8. Modern Coptic

    The Church preserves Coptic through worship, teaching, and scholarship.

Historical dialects

Bohairic

Historically associated with the Nile Delta and now the principal Coptic liturgical dialect.

Sahidic

A major literary dialect associated with Upper Egypt.

Akhmimic

Associated with Akhmim in Upper Egypt.

Lycopolitan

An Upper Egyptian literary variety sometimes called Subakhmimic.

Fayyumic

Associated with the Fayyum and known for distinctive spellings.

Middle Egyptian Coptic

A regional Coptic variety distinct from the ancient stage called Middle Egyptian.

Manuscripts and preservation

Biblical manuscripts, sermons, hymns, and monastic writings preserve evidence of Coptic across regions and centuries. The White Monastery tradition associated with Shenouda the Archimandrite is especially important for Sahidic literature.

Understanding the Sounds of Coptic

Greco-Bohairic

The modern church reading reflects nineteenth-century reform associated with Iryan Girgis Moftah and the influence of contemporary Greek pronunciation.

Old Bohairic

Older Bohairic teaching approaches draw on transmitted readings and historical evidence. Individual reconstructions can differ.

English sound cues are approximate. Follow the pronunciation taught in your parish. Compare readings and listen at Tasbeha.

Understanding Coptic Language Terminology

Coptic

Latest historical stage of Egyptian.

Bohairic

Principal dialect of Coptic Orthodox liturgy.

Sahidic

A major Coptic literary dialect.

Demotic

An Egyptian script and historical language stage.

Hieroglyphs

An ancient Egyptian writing system.

Hieratic

A cursive Egyptian writing system.

Dialect

A variety of a language.

Transliteration

Representing one writing system using another.

Transcription

Representing speech sounds in writing.

Phonology

The study of sound systems.

Orthography

Spelling and writing conventions.

Loanword

A word adopted from another language.

Manuscript

A handwritten text.

Codex

A book made of bound pages.

Psalmody

The singing and collection of psalms and related hymns.

Theotokos

God-bearer, a title of the Virgin Mary.

Questions About the Coptic Language

Do I need Greek or Arabic first?

You can start Coptic directly using English-language learning materials.

Which dialect should I start with?

Bohairic is practical for parish worship. Sahidic is valuable for early Coptic literature.

Who can learn Coptic?

Children, families, deacons, and adults can all take part.
