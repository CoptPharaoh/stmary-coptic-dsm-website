The Sacrament of Priesthood

What Is the Priesthood?

The Sacrament of Priesthood, also called Holy Orders, is one of the seven Holy Mysteries of the Coptic Orthodox Church. Through this sacrament, qualified men are ordained by the Church through the laying on of hands and prayer to serve Christ and His people.

The ordained ministry exists to preserve the apostolic faith, celebrate the Holy Mysteries, proclaim the Gospel, and shepherd the faithful.

Priesthood is not simply a profession or position of authority. It is a sacred calling to serve God with humility, obedience, holiness, and sacrificial love. Through apostolic succession, the ordained ministry continues in faithfulness to the apostolic foundation established by Christ.

Biblical and Apostolic Foundations

Sent by Christ to Serve His Church

Christ Sends His Apostles

"As the Father has sent Me, I also send you."

John 20:21-23

After His Resurrection, Christ commissioned His apostles and breathed upon them, saying, "Receive the Holy Spirit." He entrusted them with reconciliation and the forgiveness of sins. The Church understands this mission as foundational to apostolic ministry and her sacramental life.

The Laying on of Hands

"Do not neglect the gift that is in you, which was given to you by prophecy with the laying on of the hands of the eldership."

1 Timothy 4:14

St. Paul reminds Timothy of the spiritual gift associated with his commissioning. Ordination is a sacred act of prayer in which the Church invokes divine grace for ministry.

The Appointment of the Seven

Acts 6:1-6

The apostles appointed seven men to assist with the practical needs of the growing Christian community. After prayer, they laid their hands upon them.

This passage witnesses to ordered ministry and service in the early Church. It does not, by itself, establish every detail of the later Coptic hierarchy of diaconal ranks.

Appointing Elders

"Set in order the things that are lacking, and appoint elders in every city as I commanded you."

Titus 1:5

St. Paul instructed Titus to appoint elders for the care and organization of the Church. This reflects the development and continuity of ordained pastoral ministry in the apostolic Church.

Read also Acts 14:23; 2 Timothy 1:6; 1 Timothy 3:1-13.

The Ministry and Responsibilities of the Priest

A Shepherd of Christ's Flock

The priest serves as a spiritual father, teacher, and shepherd. His ministry should reflect Christ, the Good Shepherd.

Celebrating the Holy Mysteries

Priests celebrate the Divine Liturgy and administer the Holy Mysteries according to the authority and order of the Church. Ordination and the consecration of Holy Myron belong to the episcopal ministry and follow the Church's canonical order.

Proclaiming the Word of God

The priest teaches the Holy Scriptures and explains the Orthodox faith, guiding the faithful in the teaching received from the apostles and preserved by the Church.

Spiritual Guidance and Confession

The priest offers pastoral counsel, hears sacramental confessions according to Church authorization, and guides believers toward repentance and reconciliation with God.

Shepherding the Faithful

The priest cares for the spiritual needs of the parish, visits the sick, supports families, and encourages the faithful in their Christian lives.

A Life of Holiness and Service

The priest is called to humility, prayer, obedience, purity, patience, and sacrificial love. His conduct should bear witness to the Lord whom he serves.

"I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep."

John 10:11

In 1 Peter 5:2-4, shepherds are called to care willingly for God's flock, serving as examples rather than lording authority over others.

The Ranks of Ordained Ministry

Holy Orders in the Coptic Orthodox Church

The three principal ordained orders are bishop, presbyter (priest), and deacon. Additional ranks, titles, and liturgical degrees express particular responsibilities within the Coptic tradition.

Bishop: Episcopos

The bishop has pastoral oversight of a diocese or other ecclesiastical jurisdiction. Bishops preserve the apostolic faith, oversee clergy, guide the faithful, and ordain clergy according to the Church's canonical order. Episcopal ministry is central to apostolic succession.

Presbyter: Priest

The priest serves under his bishop's authority. He celebrates the Divine Liturgy, administers the Holy Mysteries within his priestly authority, teaches the faith, and provides pastoral care.

Hegumen: Senior Priest

Hegumen is an ecclesiastical rank or dignity associated with senior priests and certain monastic responsibilities. It is not a separate principal sacramental order above the priesthood.

Deacon: Diakonos

Deacon means servant or minister. Deacons assist the bishop and priest in the worship and service of the Church. The Coptic tradition also recognizes several diaconal and liturgical ranks with distinct responsibilities.

Traditional Coptic Liturgical Ranks

  1. Epsaltos: Chanter.
  2. Anagnostis: Reader.
  3. Epideacon: Subdeacon.
  4. Diaconos: Deacon.
  5. Archdeacon: Chief Deacon.

These traditional ranks are not all equivalent to the major sacramental order of deacon. Their responsibilities and qualifications differ according to rank and Church regulations.

The Church Fathers on Priesthood

Humility Before a Sacred Responsibility

These attributed summaries draw on the Fathers' writings about pastoral responsibility, holiness, and Christ's mission to His Church.

St. John Chrysostom

Chrysostom describes the extraordinary spiritual responsibility of priests who serve on earth in mysteries entrusted by God. This sacred ministry calls for spiritual vigilance, purity, humility, and careful pastoral care, rather than ambition for status.

On the Priesthood, Book III, especially sections 4-6

Read the source

St. Gregory the Theologian

Gregory explains the seriousness of guiding souls and the spiritual preparation it requires. Those entrusted with leadership must first seek purification and growth in virtue so that their ministry helps others draw near to God.

Oration 2: In Defense of His Flight to Pontus, especially section 71

Read the source

St. Cyril of Alexandria

In his commentary on Christ sending the apostles, Cyril presents their mission as dependent on the Lord and the gift of the Holy Spirit. This teaching helps us understand ordained ministry as service to the Church's communion with Christ and the faithful transmission of apostolic teaching.

Commentary on John, Book XII, on John 20:21-23

Apostolic Succession

From Christ to the Apostles

The Coptic Orthodox Church understands its ordained ministry as continuing the apostolic mission entrusted by Christ to His apostles. The apostles appointed and ordained successors to preserve the faith and shepherd the growing Church.

Through episcopal ordination and the laying on of hands, the Church maintains continuity with apostolic ministry.

St. Mark and the Church of Alexandria

According to Coptic Orthodox tradition, St. Mark the Evangelist established the Church of Alexandria in the first century. The Coptic Orthodox patriarchate traces its apostolic heritage to him.

Apostolic succession involves more than an uninterrupted historical list of bishops. It requires faithfulness to apostolic teaching, sacramental life, and the communion of the Church.

Ordination and the Laying on of Hands

A Sacred Calling

Ordination is performed through the Church's liturgical prayers and the laying on of hands by the bishop, according to the requirements of the particular order. Episcopal ordinations involve bishops acting together according to canonical order.

Divine Calling

Ordained ministry is a vocation of service rather than a personal entitlement.

The Laying on of Hands

The Church invokes God's grace upon the candidate for the responsibilities of ministry.

Prayer and Consecration

The ordination service expresses the Church's dependence upon the Holy Spirit.

Obedience and Responsibility

The ordained minister serves within the Church's canonical order and under appropriate ecclesiastical authority.

Service to the Faithful

Ordination exists for the spiritual care and building up of Christ's Body.

Marriage and Celibacy in the Priesthood

Two Vocations of Service

Married Priests

The Coptic Orthodox Church permits the ordination of eligible married men under its canonical requirements. Marriage must ordinarily take place before priestly ordination.

A married priest remains faithful to his wife and family while fulfilling his pastoral responsibilities. Marriage alone does not establish eligibility for ordination.

Monastic and Celibate Clergy

Monks embrace celibacy, prayer, obedience, and spiritual discipline. In the Coptic Orthodox tradition, bishops are selected from eligible celibate monastic clergy according to canonical regulations.

Being unmarried does not automatically make a priest a monk; monastic life is a distinct vocation.

Marriage and monasticism are respected paths of Christian discipleship. Clergy may be called to different forms of service while sharing the responsibility to live faithfully before God.

Priesthood as Sacrificial Service

Following Christ the Good Shepherd

"I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep."

John 10:11

The priest imitates Christ through humility, self-denial, and service. His calling takes shape in ordinary acts of faithful care:

  • Teaching the faithful.
  • Praying for the Church.
  • Comforting those who suffer.
  • Visiting the sick.
  • Guiding those seeking repentance.
  • Protecting the apostolic faith.
  • Encouraging spiritual growth.
  • Serving without seeking worldly recognition.

Ordained ministry exists to serve the faithful and glorify God, not to elevate the minister above others.

Final Spiritual Reflection

Called to Serve Christ

The Sacrament of Priesthood is a sacred calling through which Christ continues to shepherd His Church. Ordained ministry is not simply a career or position of honor: it is a lifelong responsibility of prayer, sacrifice, teaching, and spiritual service.

Through the ministry of bishops, priests, and deacons, the Church continues its apostolic mission of proclaiming the Gospel and guiding the faithful toward communion with God.

"And no man takes this honor to himself, but he who is called by God, just as Aaron was."

Hebrews 5:4
