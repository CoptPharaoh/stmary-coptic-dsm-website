What Is a Deacon?

Diaconal ministry expresses the Church’s call to service. It brings together worship, care for others, prayer, reverence, and obedience. Study supports formation. The bishop and parish priest direct ecclesiastical service and permissions.

History of the Diaconate

New Testament accounts connect early Christian ministry with practical service and the proclamation of the Gospel. Later church orders describe liturgical responsibilities in greater detail. The Alexandrian tradition handed on distinct ranks of service, preserved in Coptic Orthodox practice today.

The Five Traditional Ranks

The first three ranks are traditionally distinguished from the ordained diaconate. A vestment or parish convention alone does not identify someone’s rank.

Epsaltos · Chanter

Learns and leads assigned hymns and responses.

Anagnostes · Reader

Reads assigned Scripture and supports the Church’s sung worship.

Epidiacon · Subdeacon

Assists with liturgical order under clergy direction.

Diacon · Deacon

Assists the bishop or priest in worship and serves the faithful according to ecclesiastical direction.

Archdeacon · Chief deacon

Coordinates diaconal service when entrusted with that responsibility.

Ordination and Ecclesiastical Authority

The bishop and parish priest guide preparation and assignments. A deacon assists in the Eucharistic liturgy. Its celebration belongs to a bishop or priest. This learning pathway is for study and carries no ecclesiastical permissions.

St. Stephen and Other Examples of Service

St. Stephen the Archdeacon

Acts 6–7 presents Stephen as one of the Seven and recounts his witness and martyrdom. His final prayer is an example of forgiveness.

St. Philip the Evangelist

One of the Seven, remembered for proclaiming Christ in Acts 8.

St. Lawrence of Rome

Remembered as a Roman deacon and martyr.

St. Ephrem the Syrian

Remembered for diaconal service and Syriac hymns.

St. Athanasius the Apostolic

Served as a deacon before becoming bishop of Alexandria.

Spiritual Life and Practical Conduct

Prepare through prayer, Scripture, repentance, confession, and Eucharistic participation under pastoral guidance. Practice humility, obedience, and love in the way you serve and correct others.

Before the service

Arrive ready, review the assigned texts, and check directions with the leader.

During the service

Listen, respond attentively, and follow the priest and hymn leader.

After the service

Help as directed and note a passage or hymn to review.

Serving the Divine Liturgy

  1. Preparation and Raising of Incense

    Follow the responses for the service of incense.

  2. Offering of the Lamb

    Listen for the appointed hymns during the offering.

  3. Liturgy of the Word

    Prepare assigned readings beforehand.

  4. Gospel and sermon

    Stand attentively and follow the Gospel response.

  5. Prayer of Reconciliation

    Listen for the call to reconciliation.

  6. Anaphora

    Follow the dialogue that opens the Eucharistic prayer.

  7. Sanctus and Eucharistic prayers

    Join the appointed praise with the congregation.

  8. Litanies and commemorations

    Listen to the petitions and commemorations.

  9. Fraction and preparation for Communion

    Follow the appointed responses as the priest prays the Fraction.

  10. Holy Communion

    Follow the clergy’s direction and learn the Communion hymns.

  11. Thanksgiving and dismissal

    Remain attentive through the final thanksgiving and blessing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a deacon celebrate the Liturgy independently?

Celebration belongs to a bishop or priest.

Does training confer ordination?

Website study prepares learners for discussion with clergy. Ordination is an ecclesiastical act.

Can girls and women learn the hymns?

Anyone can learn and sing the Church’s hymns. Clergy guide specific liturgical assignments.

Do I need to know Coptic first?

Ask your instructor for an appropriate starting lesson.

What if I make a mistake?

Keep listening, rejoin at the next familiar phrase, and ask about it after the service.

Where can I practice at home?

Follow the weekly sheet and the audio links above.
